# The Anatomy of a Modern Magic: The Gathering Set

A comprehensive structural reference for designing a draftable Magic set. Every number, ratio, and structural element a designer needs to know, verified against Mark Rosewater's Nuts & Bolts series (#13, #15, #16) and current set data.

---

## 1. Total Set Size and Rarity Breakdown

### Draft Booster Era (Pre-2024)

The standard large set contained **261 unique cards** distributed as follows:

| Rarity | Count | Notes |
|--------|-------|-------|
| Common | 101 | 19 per color + 6 artifacts |
| Uncommon | 80 | 13 per color + 10 multicolor (signpost cycle) + 5 artifacts |
| Rare | 60 | ~9-10 per color + multicolor/colorless/lands |
| Mythic Rare | 20 | ~3 per color + multicolor/colorless |
| **Total** | **261** | |

Additional components not counted in the 261: 5 basic lands (one per type), tokens (varies, typically 10-20).

### Play Booster Era (2024-Present)

The shift to Play Boosters changed the common/uncommon balance while keeping the total at **261 unique cards**:

| Rarity | Count | Change from Draft Booster |
|--------|-------|---------------------------|
| Common | 81 | -20 |
| Uncommon | 100 | +20 |
| Rare | 60 | No change |
| Mythic Rare | 20 | No change |
| **Total** | **261** | |

Where the 20 new uncommon slots went (per N&B #16): 5 went to monocolor (one per color), 10 went to multicolor (one more signpost per two-color pair, bringing the total to 20 multicolor uncommons), and 5 went to colorless (artifacts or lands).

### Recent Set Sizes (2025)

Recent 2025 sets have been slightly larger than the 261 baseline, with total base set counts around **291 cards** (e.g., Aetherdrift: 23 mythics, 60 rares, 100 uncommons, 96 commons; Tarkir: Dragonstorm: 25 mythics, 66 rares, 100 uncommons, 96 commons). The increased commons include reprints; the number of new common designs remains close to the ~81 target.

**Source:** [Nuts & Bolts #13](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/nuts-bolts-13-design-skeleton-revisited-2021-03-22), [Nuts & Bolts #16](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/nuts-and-bolts-16-play-boosters)

## 2. Color Distribution

### Commons

**Draft Booster era:** 19 cards per color, 6 artifacts (101 total).

**Play Booster era:** The total is 81, with the per-color distribution shifting. Based on the creature counts and percentages given in N&B #16:

| Color | Total Common Cards (Play Booster) |
|-------|-----------------------------------|
| White | 15 |
| Blue | 15 |
| Black | ~14-15 |
| Red | 14 |
| Green | 14 |
| Artifacts/Colorless | ~6 |
| Common Lands | ~3-5 (color-producing) |

Note: The exact per-color count varies slightly by set. The figures above are derived from the creature counts and percentages stated in N&B #16 (e.g., White has 11 creatures out of 15 cards = 73%).

### Uncommons

**Draft Booster era (80 total):**
- 13 cards per color (65 monocolor)
- 10 multicolor signpost cards (one per two-color pair)
- 5 artifacts

**Play Booster era (100 total):**
- 14 cards per color (70 monocolor)
- 20 multicolor signpost cards (two per two-color pair)
- 10 artifacts/colorless/lands

### Rares (60 total)

Approximately **9-10 rares per monocolor**, with the remaining slots allocated to multicolor, colorless/artifact, and land cards. Rares are always color-balanced. In a multicolor-themed set, per-color counts decrease to make room for gold cards.

### Mythic Rares (20 total)

Approximately **3 per monocolor**, with remaining slots for multicolor, colorless, and/or land cards. Planeswalkers are almost always mythic rare.

**Source:** [Nuts & Bolts #13](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/nuts-bolts-13-design-skeleton-revisited-2021-03-22), [Nuts & Bolts #16](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/nuts-and-bolts-16-play-boosters)

## 3. Creature vs. Non-Creature Ratio

The overall target is approximately **50% creatures** across the set. Each color has its own ratio, and the order is always: White (most creatures) > Green > Black > Red > Blue (fewest creatures).

### Common Creature Percentages

The percentages changed significantly from Draft Booster to Play Booster:

| Color | Draft Booster % | Draft Booster Count (of 19) | Play Booster % | Play Booster Count |
|-------|----------------|----------------------------|----------------|-------------------|
| White | 63% | 12 | 73% | 11 of 15 |
| Blue | 47% | 9 | 53% | 8 of 15 |
| Black | 52% | 10 | 62% | 9 of ~14 |
| Red | 53% | 10 | 64% | 9 of 14 |
| Green | 58% | 11 | 71% | 10 of 14 |

Note on the Draft Booster figures: N&B #13 states "62% for White, dropping 3% per step" which gives 62/59/56/53/50. The actual creature-per-slot counts in the skeleton are 12/9/10/10/11. The percentages cited in N&B #16 as the "old" numbers (63/47/52/53/58) differ slightly because they were calculated differently. Both sets of numbers are from Rosewater; the skeleton slot counts (12/9/10/10/11 out of 19) are the concrete design targets.

### Uncommon Creature Percentages

**Draft Booster era (13 cards per color):**

| Color | Creatures | Non-Creatures | % |
|-------|-----------|--------------|---|
| White | 8 | 5 | 61% |
| Blue | 7 | 6 | 54% |
| Black | 7 | 6 | 54% |
| Red | 7 | 6 | 54% |
| Green | 8 | 5 | 61% |
| Multicolor | 10 | 0 | 100% (signpost creatures) |
| Artifacts | 3 creatures + 2 non-creatures | | 60% |

**Play Booster era (14 cards per color):** Creature percentages generally increased, with White and Green going up the most. Blue is the only color where the uncommon creature percentage went down (from 54% to ~46%).

### Rare and Mythic Rare

Creature ratios at rare and mythic are more flexible. Roughly 50% of rares are creatures. Mythic rares include a higher proportion of non-creature splashy spells and planeswalkers, so the creature percentage is lower (typically ~40-50%).

**Source:** [Nuts & Bolts #13](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/nuts-bolts-13-design-skeleton-revisited-2021-03-22), [Nuts & Bolts #16](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/nuts-and-bolts-16-play-boosters)

### Uncommon Creature Curves

The creature curve is more flexible at uncommon. Each color has **two "any MV" slots** that can be placed wherever the set needs them. The constraint is that no more than two creatures at uncommon should share the same mana value.

| Blue | 7 | 1-2 through Any (two flex slots) |
| Black | 7 | 1-2 through Any (two flex slots) |
| Red | 7 | 1-2 through 5+ (two flex slots) |
| Green | 8 | 1-2 through 6+ (two flex slots) |

## 5. Creature Sizing

Every creature in the design skeleton falls into one of three size categories:

| Size | Power/Toughness Range |
|------|----------------------|
| Small | 0/1 to 2/3 |
| Medium | 3/3 to 4/5 |
| Large | 5/5 and above |

### Distribution by Color at Common (Draft Booster Era)

Each color has a characteristic size profile. White beats you with many small creatures; green beats you with fewer but larger ones.

| Color | Small | Medium | Large | Profile |
|-------|-------|--------|-------|---------|
| White | 5-6 | 4 | 1-2 | Skews heavily small ("the army color") |
| Blue | 4-5 | 3 | 1-2 | Skews small; top-end creatures often have attacking restrictions or tap abilities |
| Black | 3-4 | 4 | 2-3 | Balanced across sizes |
| Red | 4-5 | 3-4 | 1-2 | Skews small-to-medium; aggressive curve |
| Green | 2-3 | 4-5 | 3-4 | Skews medium-to-large; gets the biggest creatures |

In the Play Booster era, average creature power has been pushed up across all colors (see table in Section 4), meaning the medium and large categories have expanded slightly.

### Uncommon and Above

At uncommon and higher rarities, creatures can be larger and more impactful. Uncommon creatures are more likely to have multiple keywords. The size distribution is less rigid — the two flex mana-value slots at uncommon let designers place creatures wherever the set needs them.

At rare, creatures are often either very efficient (strong stats for low cost) or very large and splashy. At mythic, creatures should create memorable gameplay moments and feel qualitatively different from rares.

**Source:** [Nuts & Bolts #13](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/nuts-bolts-13-design-skeleton-revisited-2021-03-22)

## 7. Booster Pack Contents

### Play Booster Composition (2024-Present)

Play Boosters replaced both Draft Boosters and Set Boosters starting with Murders at Karlov Manor (February 2024). Each pack contains **14 playable cards + 1 non-playable card**:

| Slot | Contents | Details |
|------|----------|---------|
| 1-6 | 6 Common cards | From the main set. At least 4 of the 5 colors are guaranteed; usually all 5 are represented. |
| 7 | Common / The List | 87.5% of the time: a common from the set. 12.5% (1 in 8 packs): a card from The List (~9.38% common/uncommon List cards, ~1.56% rare/mythic List cards, ~1.56% Special Guest cards). |
| 8-10 | 3 Uncommon cards | From the main set. |
| 11 | 1 Rare or Mythic Rare | Guaranteed. Each rare slot has a 1-in-7 chance of being a mythic rare instead. |
| 12 | 1 Land card | Basic land or common non-basic land (set-dependent). |
| 13 | 1 Non-foil Wildcard | Can be any rarity from the main set (common through mythic rare). |
| 14 | 1 Traditional Foil Wildcard | Can be any rarity from the main set. Guaranteed foil. |
| 15 | 1 Art Card or Play Aid | Non-playable. Not used in Limited. |

### Key Pack Statistics

- **Rares per pack:** 1-4 possible. The guaranteed rare (slot 11) + wildcard slots (13 and 14) can each independently be rare or mythic.
- **Multiple rare probability:** ~37% chance of 2+ rares/mythics, ~4% chance of 3, <1% chance of 4.
- **Mythic rare odds:** 1 in 7 per rare slot.
- **Rares per box (36 packs):** Approximately 5-6 mythic rares from guaranteed rare slots alone, plus whatever appears in wildcard slots.
- **Foil guarantee:** Every pack contains exactly one traditional foil (slot 14).
- **Draftable cards:** 13 per pack (14 minus the non-playable slot 15; the land in slot 12 is draftable).

### Draft Booster Composition (Pre-2024, for Reference)

| Slot | Contents |
|------|----------|
| 1-10 | 10 Common cards |
| 11-13 | 3 Uncommon cards |
| 14 | 1 Rare or Mythic Rare |
| 15 | 1 Basic Land (sometimes foil) |

Draft Boosters had 15 cards, 14 playable + 1 basic land. No wildcard slots, no guaranteed foil (foils replaced a common slot ~1 in 3 packs).

### 2025 Box Size Change

Starting with Aetherdrift (2025), Play Booster boxes contain **30 packs** instead of 36.

**Source:** [Wizards: What Are Play Boosters](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/what-are-play-boosters), [MTG Wiki: Play Booster](https://mtg.fandom.com/wiki/Play_Booster), [Nuts & Bolts #16](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/nuts-and-bolts-16-play-boosters)

## 8. Mechanic Budget

### Named/Keyword Mechanics

A premier set typically contains **3-6 named keyword mechanics**. This is the budget for mechanics that get their own keyword or ability word (e.g., Flashback, Revolt, Connive).

- Supplemental sets (e.g., Modern Horizons) may have many more mechanics.
- Not every mechanic needs to appear at common. Some are uncommon-and-above only.
- Each mechanic must have enough design space to fill its allocated slots. If a mechanic can't produce at least 8-10 interesting cards across rarities, it may not justify its keyword.

### Unnamed Mechanics/Themes

In addition to named mechanics, each set contains **1-4 unnamed mechanics** — recurring patterns that appear on multiple cards but don't receive a keyword. These typically appear on **2-5 cards each**.

### "_______ Matters" Theme

Every set has a thematic throughline (e.g., "artifacts matter," "graveyard matters," "enchantments matter," "instants and sorceries matter"). This is the connective tissue that ties mechanics together.

### Mechanic Concentration

Mechanics should be concentrated in fewer colors to increase their density (as-fan) where they appear, rather than spread thinly across all five colors. Use existing planned cards to do double duty supporting new mechanics.

### As-Fan Calculation

As-fan measures how frequently a mechanic appears in an average booster pack. Key guidelines:
- If as-fan is too low, the mechanic won't feel like a set theme.
- If as-fan is too high, it crowds out other mechanics.
- Uncommons count as roughly half a common for as-fan calculations.
- Getting the as-fan right is one of the hardest structural challenges in set design.

**Source:** [Nuts & Bolts #15](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/nuts-and-bolts-15-structural-support)

## 9. Multicolor Card Slots

### Uncommon Signpost Cards

Multicolor uncommons are the structural backbone of draft archetypes.

**Draft Booster era:** 10 multicolor uncommons total — one signpost creature per two-color pair (UZ01-UZ10). Each signpost communicates what its draft archetype is trying to do.

**Play Booster era:** 20 multicolor uncommons total — two per two-color pair. This was the single biggest structural change from the format shift. The two cards serve distinct roles:
1. **Enabler:** Helps make the archetype work (e.g., provides the right kind of card selection or board development).
2. **Payoff:** A strong reward for doing what the archetype wants you to do (e.g., a powerful effect that triggers when the archetype's condition is met).

In the Draft Booster world, designers had to choose which of these two functions the single gold signpost served. In the Play Booster world, there is space for both.

### Multicolor at Other Rarities

- **Common:** Multicolor cards at common are rare in standard sets. They appear primarily in multicolor-focused sets (e.g., Ravnica, New Capenna). Most standard sets have 0 multicolor commons.
- **Rare:** Typically 5-10 multicolor rares, often including cycle cards.
- **Mythic Rare:** 2-5 multicolor mythics, often including flagship multicolor planeswalkers or legendary creatures.

### Draft Archetype Support Structure

Per N&B #15, each two-color draft archetype should have at least **6 dedicated support cards**:
- 2 monocolor commons (one in each color)
- 2 monocolor uncommons (one in each color)
- 2 multicolor uncommons (the signpost pair in Play Booster era)

### Archetype Novelty Distribution

Of the 10 two-color archetypes in a set (per N&B #15):
- **2 archetypes** should be completely novel — something players haven't seen before
- **4 archetypes** should have slight variations on familiar themes
- **4 archetypes** should be familiar, comfortable designs players recognize

**Source:** [Nuts & Bolts #15](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/nuts-and-bolts-15-structural-support), [Nuts & Bolts #16](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/nuts-and-bolts-16-play-boosters)

## 10. Land Slots

### Basic Lands

Every set includes the 5 basic land types (Plains, Island, Swamp, Mountain, Forest). These are not counted in the 261-card total. The booster pack land slot (slot 12 in Play Boosters) contains a basic land or, in some sets, a common non-basic land.

### Non-Basic Land Cycles

A typical modern standard set contains approximately **15-25 non-basic lands** across all rarities, distributed across cycles:

**Rare dual land cycle (5-10 cards):**
- Most sets include one cycle of rare dual lands, either covering all 10 two-color pairs or split 5/5 across sets (allied pairs in one set, enemy pairs in the next).
- Examples: Pain lands (tap for colorless or 1 life for colored), fast lands (enter untapped if you control 2 or fewer other lands), check lands (enter untapped if you control the right basic).

**Uncommon dual land cycle (5-10 cards):**
- Typically enters-the-battlefield-tapped with a small bonus (e.g., gain 1 life, surveil 1).
- These are the primary source of mana fixing in Limited play.
- Example: Surveil lands from Murders at Karlov Manor (uncommon, have basic land types).

**Common lands (0-5 cards):**
- Common non-basic lands appear in some sets. The Play Booster land slot can hold a common non-basic land, making this slot set-dependent.

**Utility lands (various rarities, 2-5 cards):**
- Creature lands, legendary lands, colorless utility lands (e.g., Demolition Field, Cavern of Souls).
- These don't always form complete cycles.

### Mana Fixing Philosophy

From N&B #15 on structural infrastructure:
- **Mana fixing is necessary** for Limited to function. Without it, two-color decks become unreliable and players are punished for drafting the signpost uncommons.
- **Be cautious with 3+ color lands.** Overly efficient multi-color fixing leads to "goodstuff" drafting where players ignore archetypes and just take the best cards in any color. This undermines the entire archetype structure.
- **Uncommon dual lands** are the sweet spot for Limited fixing — they're common enough to appear regularly but not so common that every deck has perfect mana.
- **Common fixing** (if present) should be weak or conditional (enters tapped, no bonus).

### Land Slots in the Design Skeleton

From N&B #13, the design skeleton allocates approximately 5 common slots to lands (color-producing). At uncommon, non-basic lands use some of the 5 artifact/colorless slots. At rare, a cycle of 5 or 10 dual lands is standard.

**Source:** [Nuts & Bolts #13](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/nuts-bolts-13-design-skeleton-revisited-2021-03-22), [Nuts & Bolts #15](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/nuts-and-bolts-15-structural-support), [Nuts & Bolts #16](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/nuts-and-bolts-16-play-boosters)

## Structural Infrastructure Checklist

Every set needs these invisible support structures (per N&B #15):

- [ ] **Mana fixing** — Dual lands at uncommon minimum; be cautious with 3+ color lands
- [ ] **Mana sinks** — Things to do with excess mana in the late game
- [ ] **Deck smoothing** — Card draw, scry, cycling, or equivalent to reduce flood/screw
- [ ] **Evasion abilities** — Flying, menace, trample distributed across colors to break board stalls
- [ ] **Removal suite** — Balanced ecosystem of creature removal at common and uncommon (White: efficient removal spell at uncommon; Blue: counterspell + bounce; Black: destruction removal; Red: direct damage, including one any-target and one multi-target at uncommon; Green: fight/bite effects, artifact/enchantment destruction)
- [ ] **Threats and answers** — Bombs exist at rare/mythic, but answers exist at lower rarities
- [ ] **Color-specific non-creature role slots:**
  - Blue uncommon: Card draw spell + Counterspell
  - Black uncommon: Removal spell + Reanimation effect
  - Red uncommon: Any-target direct damage + Small sweeper/multi-target damage
  - Green uncommon: Mana ramp spell (stronger than common ramp)
