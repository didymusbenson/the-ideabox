# Unbelievable — World Building

## The Plane

**Name:** Shandalark

**Why Shandalark:** Evokes Shandalar — the existing MTG plane that was never given a dedicated set, canonically mana-rich, kitchen-sink high fantasy, and designed by WotC as the "baseline Magic world." The added "k" makes it original while keeping the Magic DNA. "Lark" = a fun adventure, fitting the isekai tone. Inherits Shandalar's aesthetic (rogue plane, abundant mana, classic creature types) without inheriting its continuity obligations.

**Previous names:** ~~Shandalar~~ (holding name), ~~Ahndil~~ (Celtic/Sumerian — abandoned, real-world mythology doesn't serve this set).

**Logline (7 words or less):**
"Magic players rule an isekai plane." <!-- 6 words -->

**Genre / Source Material:**
Isekai (Japanese portal fantasy — characters transported to another world and empowered by knowledge from their origin). Touchstones: *Sword Art Online*, *Overlord*, *That Time I Got Reincarnated as a Slime*, *Log Horizon*. The twist: the transported individuals are Magic: The Gathering players, and their knowledge of the game's systems *is* their power in this world.

**Aesthetic Direction:** Classic, unapologetic high fantasy in the vein of Dominaria — not filtered through any single Earth mythology. The plane should feel like *Magic: The Gathering itself*. Elves, Goblins, Angels, Dragons, Merfolk — the full creature type palette players expect. The world is the game made real. This isn't generic — it's deliberately the most Magic-feeling plane possible, because that's what makes the arrivals' knowledge meaningful.

## The World

### Core Concept
A plane that operates under the literal rules of Magic: The Gathering — five colors of mana, the color pie's mechanical truths, all of it real. Natives live in this world without fully understanding its underlying system. Then humans from *our* world start arriving — isekai'd through various means — and some of them are Magic players. They recognize the system. They know the rules. They exploit that knowledge to gain power at extraordinary speed.

The set's timeline **spans from the first arrivals through to a major magical conflict** between the players. Cards depict different moments across this arc — early arrivals finding their footing, the players discovering and exploiting the system, their growing specialization in their color combinations, accumulating power, and the escalation into open conflict.

The five players' stories are told through the cards themselves. Commons and uncommons document their rise to power — a player's early, uncertain cards at lower rarities evolving into their fully realized legendary form. A card like "Fresh Arrival" might depict a human who just got here; cards deeper in a character's color identity show what happens when they've learned to wield this world's magic.

### Shandalark the Wandering Plane

Shandalark is a wandering plane — like its namesake Shandalar, it drifts unpredictably through the Blind Eternities, brushing against other planes as it moves. This wandering is ancient, and it has shaped everything about the world.

**The Cycle of Import:** When Shandalark faces a major threat, its wild mana responds by reaching out to nearby planes and pulling in outsiders. This is a defense mechanism — the plane itself recruiting help when its native inhabitants can't handle what's coming. The cycle has repeated many times across Shandalark's history, and each time it leaves its mark on the world.

**This cycle explains why Shandalark feels like "classic Magic."** The plane's creature palette — Dragons, Dinosaurs, Slivers, and countless other types — isn't native to a single evolutionary history. It's the accumulated result of cosmic immigration over millennia. Every time Shandalark brushed against another plane and activated its cycle, it imported creatures, cultures, and magic from that world. Some stayed. Some became foundational. The Dragons that Vorthos befriends may have arrived centuries ago from another plane entirely. Shandalark feels like every Magic plane because it has *literally collected pieces of them*.

Past cycles have produced their own heroes — figures now remembered only in Birdkind's histories and the Dragons' memories. A Sliver warrior from when the plane drifted near a Sliver-dominated world. A Kor shaman from another brush. These are ancient, one-off legends, proof the cycle has been running for ages.

### The Current Cycle — Earth

Shandalark's current drift has brought it into proximity with Earth. The cycle has activated — not because Earth triggered it, but because a threat on Shandalark demands it. The plane's proximity to Earth determines *who* gets pulled in (humans), but the *reason* is Shandalark's own danger.

**The three isekai vehicles represent a spectrum of intentionality:**
- **Portals** — The plane's wild mana acting on instinct, creating dimensional rifts that pull in whoever happens to be nearby. The least targeted method. Casts a wide net.
- **Summoning** — Birdkind performing deliberate rituals to call specific outsiders. They understand the cycle and know how to work with the plane's magic to recruit intentionally. The most targeted method.
- **Truck-kun (the 2016 Isuzu Elf)** — The plane intercepting dying or near-death souls from Earth and reincarnating them on Shandalark. The classic isekai vehicle: you die on Earth, you wake up here. The plane is scavenging souls that are already in transition.

The arrivals are not all Magic players. The plane casts a wide net, and plenty of ordinary humans have shown up — people like Dave Gibbs, CPA, who have no special framework for understanding this world's magic. They're just... here. The Magic players, however, recognized the system immediately and leveraged that knowledge into rapid power acquisition.

### The Planar Threat

Something is threatening Shandalark — something significant enough to trigger the import cycle. Mel is the only arrival who has recognized this. While the other four players make power moves, Mel is trying to master the plane's real magic and prepare for what's coming.

### Timeline Arc (Told Through the Cards)

The set doesn't depict a single moment — it spans the full arc:

1. **Arrival** — Humans appear on Shandalark, confused and vulnerable. Portals, summonings, truck-kun. The plane is casting its net. (Commons depicting fresh arrivals, early survival, ordinary humans finding their footing.)
2. **Discovery** — The Magic players realize the world runs on MTG rules. They begin experimenting, testing the system, confirming that what they know from the card game is real here. (Commons/uncommons showing early mastery, the "gaming HUD" moment.)
3. **Transformation** — The arrivals commit to their paths. Each player gravitates toward their colors and begins accumulating real power. This is where Level Up lives — the journey from newcomer to force of nature. This is where Forsake lives — the irreversible commitment, giving up any hope of going back. The ordinary humans transform too, specializing through mana mastery. (Uncommons showing growing command of their shard's magic, the personal cost of power.)
4. **Tension** — The five players' incompatible ambitions collide. Spike consolidates Mankind into an empire. Johnny disrupts it with creative chaos. Vorthos and Timmy are entrenched in The Wilds. Mel is trying to warn everyone about the real threat while building alliances with Birdkind. The inter-player conflict dominates — but it's not the endgame. (Uncommons/rares depicting established authority, open conflict, the five-way power struggle.)
5. **Fight the Threat** — The reason the plane brought them here. The planar threat manifests, and the import cycle's purpose is revealed. Whether the five arrivals can stop fighting each other long enough to face what's coming is the set's ultimate question. (Rares/mythics depicting the climax, the threat itself, the arrivals forced to confront something bigger than their conflict.)

### Central Conflict
**Two layers of conflict, one feeding into the other:**

**Layer 1 — Players vs. Players (Tension phase):** The five arrivals have incompatible ambitions. Spike wants to rule, Johnny wants to create without limits, Vorthos wants to preserve, Timmy wants to experience, Mel wants to prepare. Months of accumulating power have brought them into open conflict. This is the set's primary drama and where most cards live.

**Layer 2 — Everyone vs. The Threat (Climax):** The planar threat that triggered the import cycle. Mel has been warning about it; the others have been too busy with their own power moves to listen. When it manifests, the question becomes: can five people with fundamentally different philosophies unite against something bigger? The import cycle brought them here for THIS — not to fight each other.

**There is no single protagonist.** The arc isn't about one player winning. Mel is the most sympathetic (she's been preparing for the real fight all along), but the story needs all five. Each arrival's psychographic lens becomes a different way of approaching the threat — Spike's optimization, Johnny's creativity, Vorthos's knowledge, Timmy's raw power, Mel's systemic understanding.

## Factions — The Five Arrivals (Shards)

The five MTG-player arrivals each align with a shard (one color + its two allies). Their enemy pair is their core identity; their two allied pairs are shared borders with adjacent players, creating zones of overlap and conflict.

### Spike — Esper (WUB)
- **Core Pair:** WB (calculated ambition)
- **Shared Borders:** WU (with Mel), UB (with Johnny)
- **Identity:** The competitive player. Sees the world as a game to be won. Values efficiency, optimization, and results above all else. Has built the most organized power structure — not out of idealism, but because structure wins.
- **Native Relationship:** Infiltrates Mankind's institutions. Co-opts the nobility, plays the political game, and seeks to consolidate the fractured human kingdoms into a single empire under his control. Builds a Wizard army from trained spellcasters — the human establishment reorganized under a competitive player's strategy.
- **Role in Conflict:** The most dangerous arrival. Treats the other players as opponents to outmaneuver. Would ally with anyone temporarily if it serves the win condition. His drive to unify Mankind puts him in direct opposition to Johnny's anarchic disruption — the Spike/Johnny conflict over human civilization is the set's central power struggle.

### Johnny — Grixis (UBR)
- **Core Pair:** UR (creative experimentation)
- **Shared Borders:** UB (with Spike), BR (with Vorthos)
- **Identity:** The creative player. Sees the world as a canvas for self-expression. Drawn to unusual interactions, unexpected combos, and doing things nobody thought possible. Power through ingenuity.
- **Native Relationship:** Disrupts Mankind's order. Where Spike seeks to unify the kingdoms, Johnny resists centralization and pushes toward creative chaos and pseudo-anarchy. His Zombies are created from the human population — not mindless hordes but necromantic engineering experiments, each one a prototype. This puts him in direct conflict with Spike over the fate of human civilization.
- **Role in Conflict:** The wildcard. Unpredictable because Johnny doesn't optimize — Johnny innovates. Dangerous not because of raw power but because you never know what Johnny will do next. ### The Natives — Three Pre-Arrival Powers

Before the arrivals, Shandalark was shaped by three coexisting powers. They were not at war — they simply existed, each occupying their own space with occasional tensions but no open conflict.

#### Mankind
The human civilizations of Shandalark. Multiple kingdoms, independent cities, and scattered villages — **not a unified whole**. Mankind's internal politics are their primary source of conflict: rival kings, noble houses jockeying for influence, trade disputes between cities. Think age-of-discovery era with magical influence — developing industry, early technology, expanding populations. **Artifacts are primarily Mankind's wheelhouse** — their ingenuity expresses through crafted objects, tools, weapons, and machines. A few historically significant artifacts exist among the Dragons and Birdkind, but the bulk of artifact production and artifact-matters design belongs to human civilization.

Mankind has been expanding as its population grows, pushing into territory that borders The Wilds and Birdkind's domains. This creates low-level tension but hasn't escalated to war. The existing nobility and political structures provide the framework that Spike and Johnny both interact with — in opposite ways.

#### Birdkind — The Larkians
The bird-people of Shandalark — a civilization of humanoid-bird hybrids (aarakocra/Rito-style avian people) alongside the plane's natural birds. More connected to nature and the plane's natural magics than Mankind. **Enchantments are primarily Birdkind's wheelhouse** — their magic expresses through persistent magical effects woven into the world itself, not crafted objects.

Birdkind holds ancient knowledge about Shandalark's true nature — including the import cycle. They understand how and why the plane summons outsiders, and they know how to perform the summoning rituals deliberately. This makes them the keepers of the plane's deepest history, and likely the faction most aware that a threat is coming. Their understanding of the cycle is why Mel connects with them — the arrival who appreciates systems allies with the faction that comprehends the most fundamental system on the plane.

#### The Wilds
The untamed regions of Shandalark — home to the Dragons and the Dinosaurs, and to the human tribes who left the kingdoms behind to live alongside these creatures. The Wilds are not a political entity — they're a biome, a way of life, and a territory that the other two powers respect (or at least don't encroach on too aggressively).

**The Dragons** are ancient, intelligent beings — possibly among the earliest imports from Shandalark's past cycles. They hold deep knowledge of the plane's history and nature. They are not a unified nation but individual powers, each with their own territory and temperament. Vorthos earns their trust through genuine curiosity about their knowledge, forming an uneasy alliance built on shared love of the plane's stories.

**The Dinosaurs** are primal, powerful creatures that dominate the wildest regions. **Human tribes** who rejected the kingdoms live alongside them — frontier people, nomads, and tribal communities who chose raw experience over political civilization. Timmy joins these tribes, finding kinship with people who value the same things he does: spectacle, power, living in the moment.

### How the Arrivals Reshaped the World

The pre-arrival world had three powers in rough equilibrium. The five arrivals fractured that balance into five spheres of influence:

- **Spike** infiltrates Mankind's institutions — co-opts the nobility, plays the political game, and works to consolidate the fractured kingdoms into a single empire under his control. He's a centralizer. Multiple kingdoms are an inefficiency he can solve.
- **Johnny** disrupts Mankind's order — resists Spike's consolidation, creates Zombies from the population, and pushes toward creative chaos and pseudo-anarchy. He's a decentralizer. Structure is a constraint on creativity. The Spike/Johnny tug-of-war over human civilization is the set's most volatile conflict.
- **Vorthos** ventures into The Wilds seeking historical answers about the plane, which leads him to the Dragons. His alliance with them is uneasy — earned through genuine curiosity, not power. He's an investigator.
- **Timmy** goes native — joins the wild human tribes who live with the Dinosaurs. He doesn't conquer them; he assimilates. He's an experience-seeker who found his people.
- **Mel** allies with Birdkind and focuses on mastering the plane's real magic in all its complexity. She's the only arrival aware of the planar threat and is trying to prepare while the others make power moves. She's a bridge-builder — the only arrival who might be constructive rather than disruptive to the existing order.

### Regular Humans (Non-Player Arrivals)

Non-Magic-players who also got isekai'd. No special framework for exploiting the world's rules — but this is still a world that's trying to bring in heroes, and the plane's magic works on everyone.

**Role in the Set:** Incidental. Their purpose is to highlight the import cycle — the plane casts a wide net, and most of what it catches are ordinary people. They're the background against which the five players' extraordinary rise stands out.

**Power-Up Rules:** The main vehicle for gaining power on Shandalark is harnessing and using mana according to Magic's rules. No multiclass min-max cheese — people who specialize in something get better at that specialty through mana mastery. A blacksmith gets supernaturally good at smithing. A healer becomes a powerful cleric. The isekai anime tropes apply: special skills emerging, the "gaming HUD" moment of understanding how the world's system works, and in some cases reincarnation effects (arrived as a human, transformed into something else entirely).

## Color Pie Alignment

How does this world express each color's philosophy? Each color appears in exactly 2 shards (2 arrivals).

- **White:** Spike (Esper) + Timmy (Naya) + Mel (Bant). Structure, rules mastery, community. The color of both the optimizer and the organizer.
- **Blue:** Spike (Esper) + Johnny (Grixis) + Mel (Bant). Knowledge, system understanding, manipulation. The most "player-knowledge" color — appears in 3 of 5 arrivals.
- **Black:** Spike (Esper) + Johnny (Grixis) + Vorthos (Jund). Ambition, willingness to pay costs, pragmatism. The color of doing whatever it takes.
- **Red:** Johnny (Grixis) + Vorthos (Jund) + Timmy (Naya). Passion, impulse, spectacle. The emotional core of the arrivals.
- **Green:** Vorthos (Jund) + Timmy (Naya) + Mel (Bant). Connection to the world, raw power, natural systems. The color most tied to the plane itself.
