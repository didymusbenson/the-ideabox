# Unbelievable — Draft Archetypes

## Archetype Structure

**Model:** 5 shard factions (one per MTG player archetype), generating 10 two-color archetypes. Enemy pairs = character core identity. Allied pairs = shared borders between adjacent characters.

**Speed Balance Target:** ~3 aggro / ~4 midrange / ~3 control (adjust as needed)

**Novelty Balance Target:** ~3-4 familiar / ~4 varied / ~2 novel

## Outstanding Design Questions

- [x] What structural model? → 5 shards (arrivals), 10 two-color archetypes from enemy pairs (core) + allied pairs (borders).
- [ ] What is the set's primary mechanical through-line that ties archetypes together?
- [ ] What are the actual archetype themes? Each enemy pair needs a theme driven by its character; each allied pair needs a theme from the blend of two characters.
- [ ] Are any archetypes tribal? If so, which creature types?
- [ ] Do any archetypes require linear support (specific cards to function) vs. modular (works independently)?
- [ ] How much cross-archetype overlap exists? Are monocolor cards serving multiple archetypes?
- [ ] Is the speed balance healthy?
- [ ] Do any two archetypes feel too similar to each other?
- [ ] The shard structure creates natural "hidden" three-color archetypes — each character's full shard. Is this intended to be draftable, or just flavor?
- [ ] Does every archetype have a clear "I'm in this archetype" signpost moment in draft?
- [ ] How do the five character legendaries work at the card level? Are they three-color (shard) cards? If so, at what rarity? They're described as "signpost legendaries" but signposts are traditionally uncommon — three-color uncommons are unusual.
